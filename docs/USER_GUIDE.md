# Heirloom User Guide

*Last updated 7 November 2026*

## Welcome to Heirloom

Heirloom is a private online home for your family's story. Think of it as a shared family album, a family tree and a memory box rolled into one, kept safely in one place where only your relatives can see it.

You do not need to be a genealogist or a computer expert. If you can send a photo on WhatsApp, you can use Heirloom. It is built for the whole family: grandparents who hold the memories, parents who want to record them, and children who will one day want to know where they came from.

With Heirloom your family can:

- Build a family tree that handles real life: second marriages, step-children, adoptions and relatives whose details nobody is sure about.
- Keep photos, letters, certificates and voice recordings together, each one tagged with the people in it.
- Write down stories and answer each other's questions, such as how grandparents first met.
- Record an elder talking about their life and let the app turn it into a draft story and suggested facts for the tree.
- See where the family has lived and moved on a map, and what happened in each decade on a timeline.
- Print a family book, download a tree chart, or take a complete copy of everything out whenever you wish.

Everything in Heirloom is private by default. Nothing is sold, and your family owns its archive. If you prefer, an admin can open the archive so that relatives can look around without an account; only members with a role can ever add or change anything.

## Getting started

You can be signed in and looking at your family archive within two minutes. There is nothing to download first.

### Creating your account

1. Open Heirloom in your web browser and choose **Start your family archive**.
2. Type your name.
3. Give an email address, a mobile number, or both. Either one can be used to sign in later, so give whichever you check most often.
4. Choose a password of at least 8 characters and type it twice.
5. Press **Create account**. It is free, and you will not be asked for a card.

If a relative sent you an invitation link, open that link first. It will take you to the same account form and then straight into their family archive.

### Signing in

On the sign-in page, enter your email address or your mobile number, then your password. Mobile numbers work with or without spaces, so +91 98765 43210 and +919876543210 are both fine.

Once you are signed in, you stay signed in on that device until you press **Sign out**. You will find Sign out at the bottom of the left-hand menu. Sign out if you share the device with people outside the family.

### Putting Heirloom on your phone

Heirloom works in any modern browser, but it is nicer as an icon on your home screen. It then opens full-screen like any other app.

- **Android (Chrome, Edge or Samsung Internet):** look for the **Install app** button at the bottom of the menu, or open the browser's own menu and choose *Install app* or *Add to Home screen*.
- **iPhone or iPad (Safari):** tap the **Share** button, then **Add to Home Screen**.

On Android you can also press and hold the Heirloom icon for shortcuts that jump straight to uploading a photo, recording an elder, or writing a story. If you lose your internet connection, the app shows a short offline page and carries on once you are back online.

### Finding your way around

The menu on the left (or behind the ☰ button on a phone) is grouped into four areas:

| Menu area | What you will find there |
| --- | --- |
| Family | Home, Family tree, People, and the "How are we related?" tool |
| Memories | Stories, Photos & files, Record an elder, Family questions |
| History | Timeline, Places & migration, Life events, Sources & evidence, Family book |
| Manage | Review queue, Members & invites, Audit history, QR code, Import & export, Settings. Only shown to editors and administrators |

Above the menu is a search box. Type part of a name, a place or a story title and Heirloom finds it, in any language or script the family has used.

At the top of the menu you will see the name of the family you are looking at. If you belong to more than one family archive, such as your own family and your spouse's, click that name to switch between them or to start a new one. A **Dark** / **Light** button near the bottom changes the colour scheme.

## Your family archive

In Heirloom everything belongs to a **family archive**, not to one person. One archive holds the whole family's tree, photos, stories and settings, and every relative you invite shares it. Most people only ever need one.

### Starting an archive

After creating your account, choose **Start a family archive**:

1. Give the family a name, for example "The Sharma Family".
2. Optionally write a line or two about where the family comes from.
3. Add yourself: your given name, surname, gender and birth date. This creates the first person in the tree and links it to your account, so Heirloom knows which box on the tree is you.
4. Press **Create archive**. You become the archive's owner.

The home page then shows a short checklist: add your parents and partner, import an existing tree if you have one, invite relatives, and record a story.

### The home page

The home page is the family noticeboard. It shows:

- Counts of people, photos, stories, documents and places, each one a shortcut.
- **Coming up**: birthdays, wedding anniversaries and remembrance days in the next 30 days.
- **On this day**: things that happened in the family on today's date in past years.
- The latest stories and photos.
- **Questions for the family** that nobody has answered yet.
- **Help complete the archive**: relatives whose profiles are missing basic details such as a birth date or a photo.
- **Recent activity**: who added or changed what.

### Inviting relatives

Administrators and the owner invite people from **Members & invites** in the menu:

1. Type the relative's email address, or leave it blank if you would rather send the link yourself on WhatsApp or by text.
2. Choose a role for them (see the table below). Contributor is the safe default for most relatives.
3. Optionally pick which person in the tree they are, so the app can say "this is you" when they arrive.
4. Press **Create invitation link**, then **Copy link** and send it to them.

The relative opens the link, creates an account or signs in, and presses **Join the family archive**. Invitation links expire after 30 days, and you can revoke one at any time if it was sent to the wrong person.

### Roles: who can do what

Every member has one role. Roles protect the archive from accidental damage while still letting everyone take part.

| Role | Best for | What they can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Viewer | Relatives who just want to look | Browse the tree, photos, stories and timeline. Cannot change anything |
| Contributor | Most family members | Add photos, stories, questions, recordings and life events straight away. Adding or changing people and relationships goes to the review queue first |
| Editor | Trusted family historians | Everything a contributor can, plus direct edits to people and relationships, deciding which version of a fact is correct, and approving or rejecting items in the review queue |
| Admin | The people running the archive | Everything an editor can, plus inviting and removing members, changing roles, family settings, the audit history, and import and export |
| Owner | The person who created the archive | Everything. There is one owner per archive |

An admin can change anyone's role later from the Members page, and can remove a member. Removing a member does not delete anything they added.

### Letting relatives look without an account

Many families have a few people who add content and a great many who only want to look. Instead of inviting everyone, an admin can open the archive to visitors: in **Manage → Settings**, tick **Open the archive to visitors** and save.

- Anyone who has the address can then browse the home page, tree, people, stories, photos, documents, timeline, places, events, sources, questions and the family book, exactly as a viewer would. Nobody needs to create an account for that.
- Visitors cannot add or change anything. Every "Add", "Edit" and "Upload" button is hidden for them, and if they open such a page directly they are asked to sign in. Recordings of elders, the review queue, members, settings, the audit history and import and export stay for members only.
- People who should add photos, stories or relatives still need an account: invite them from **Members & invites** with the role you want them to have.
- When the archive is open, the **Hide living relatives from viewers and visitors** setting applies to visitors too, so you can show the family's history while keeping living relatives private.
- Untick the box at any time to make the archive members-only again.

When the site hosts a single open archive, its front page takes visitors straight to it. With several, the front page lists them.

### Sharing the archive with a QR code

Typing a web address is fiddly, especially for older relatives. Admins can instead hand out a QR code: open **Manage → QR code** (or press **Get the QR code** in Settings). Pointing a phone camera at the code opens the family home page.

- Press **Download PNG** for a picture to drop into an invitation, a newsletter or a WhatsApp message. Pick a larger size for posters, or **Download SVG** for a code that stays sharp at any size.
- Press **Print** for a ready-made poster page with the code, the family's name and the address underneath.
- Print the code at least 3 cm wide, keep a white margin around it, and scan a printed copy with your own phone before handing it out.
- The code points at the archive's address, so it keeps working as the archive grows. If the archive is members only, people who scan it are asked to sign in; open the archive to visitors (above) if anyone with the code should be able to look around.

### The review queue

When a contributor adds a new person, edits someone's details, or links two people together, the change does not appear immediately. It waits in the **Review queue**, where an editor or admin can read it and press **Approve** or **Reject**. The contributor sees a note saying their addition will be reviewed. This keeps the tree accurate without stopping anyone from helping. Photos, stories, questions and recordings do not need review.

## The family tree

The tree is built one person at a time, always starting from someone who is already in it. You never have to draw lines yourself. Tell Heirloom that someone is a parent, child, partner or sibling of an existing person, and the tree draws itself.

### Adding a relative

1. Open the profile of someone already in the tree, for example yourself.
2. Under **Family**, choose **Add** and pick **their parent**, **their child**, **their partner / spouse** or **their sibling**.
3. Fill in what you know. Only a given name is required. Everything else can be added later.
4. Press **Add to family**. If you are a contributor, the button says **Submit for review** instead, and an editor will approve it.

If the person is already in the tree, use **Link someone already in the tree** instead of adding them again. This avoids duplicates.

### What you can record about a person

- Given name, surname and a nickname such as "Nani" or "Bade Abba".
- The name in another language or script. Names can be in Hindi, Urdu, Arabic, Bengali, Tamil, Chinese and many others, and search finds them all.
- Birth and death dates. A year alone is fine, and you can mark a date as *About*, *Before* or *After* when nobody is sure.
- Birthplace, place of death, occupation, education, religion or community, and which branch of the family they belong to.
- A written life story, or a draft of one written by the AI helper from what is already recorded.
- A profile photo, chosen from any photo they have been tagged in.

### Real families, not tidy ones

Heirloom is designed for families as they really are:

- **More than one marriage.** A person can have any number of partners. Each partnership has a type (marriage, civil union, partnership, engagement) and a status (current, divorced, separated, widowed, annulled, ended), with dates.
- **Children grouped by partnership.** On a profile, children appear under the partnership they belong to, so half-siblings are clear at a glance. Siblings are labelled full, half, step or adoptive.
- **Adoption, step-parents and guardians.** Every parent-child link has a type: biological, adoptive, step, foster, guardian or unknown.
- **Unknown parents.** If only one parent is known, the child is shown under "other parent not recorded". You can fill in the other parent later.

### Facts that people disagree about

Relatives often remember things differently. Was Dada born in 1931 or 1932? Heirloom lets you keep every version instead of arguing. On a profile, under **Facts & sources**, press **Record a fact with its source** and enter the value, where it came from (a birth certificate, Aunt Neha's memory, a gravestone), and how confident you are. An editor can then mark one value as the one to **show on the profile**. The others stay recorded with their sources. The **Sources & evidence** page lists everything the family knows and how it knows it.

### Looking at the tree

- **Family tree** in the menu shows descendants of a chosen person, grouped by each marriage or partnership. Change **Start from** and the number of **Generations** to zoom in or out. Click anyone to open their profile.
- **Ancestors** on a profile opens a classic pedigree chart of that person's parents, grandparents and so on, up to six generations. It can be downloaded as an image to share or print as a poster.
- **Life timeline** on a profile lists everything that happened in that person's life in order, with their age at each event and the births and deaths of close relatives.
- **How are we related?** takes any two people and explains the link in plain words: second cousin once removed, half-brother, aunt by marriage, step-father, and so on, with the path between them.

### Removing someone

A person can be removed from their profile with **Remove this person**. Do this carefully: it removes their links in the tree as well. If a contributor does it, the removal waits in the review queue like any other change to the tree. Photos and stories they were tagged in remain.

## Memories: photos, stories, recordings and questions

The tree is the skeleton. Memories are what make it a family. Anyone with the contributor role or above can add these straight away, without waiting for review.

### Photos, documents and recordings

Choose **Photos & files** in the menu, or **Upload photos** on the home page.

1. Pick the files, or on a phone take a photo there and then. You can choose up to 50 files at once. Photos, scanned documents as PDF, and audio or video recordings are all accepted, up to 200 MB each.
2. **Who is in it?** Start typing a name and pick people from the tree. Tagging is what connects a photo to the right profiles.
3. Add a title, a rough date such as "1958", and a place. Exact dates are not needed.
4. If the file is a document, say what kind: certificate, letter, official record, passport or ID, deed, newspaper cutting, or other.
5. Optionally put it in an **album**, such as "Nani's wedding". Albums are created on the Photos & files page.
6. Press **Upload**.

Open any photo afterwards to change its details, move through the gallery with **Prev** and **Next**, download the original, or delete it. Every photo tagged with a person appears on that person's profile under **Photos & documents**.

**AI help with photos and documents.** If the family archive has the AI helper switched on, pressing **Analyse with AI** describes the photo, estimates the year it was taken, and for a letter or certificate reads out the text so you can copy it into the transcription box. The analysis runs in the background and usually takes a minute or two; the page shows a note while it works and refreshes itself when the suggestions are ready, so you can carry on with something else meanwhile. These are suggestions to check, not facts. The AI never tries to recognise faces. Tagging who is in a picture is always done by a person.

### Stories

A story is any memory worth keeping: how the grandparents met, the year the family moved cities, a festival everyone remembers. Choose **Stories**, then **Write a story**.

- Give it a title and write it the way it is told at family gatherings. Long or short, both are fine.
- Say who told it (**As told by**), roughly when it happened, where, and which language it is written in.
- Tag the people the story is about, so it appears on their profiles.
- Optionally attach an audio recording of the story being told.

Stories can be edited or deleted later by the person who wrote them or by an editor. They also appear in the family book and on the timeline.

### Family questions

Not sure about something? Ask the whole family. Choose **Family questions**, type your question such as "How did Dada and Dadi meet?", optionally say who it is about, and press **Post question**. Open questions appear on everyone's home page. Whoever knows presses **Answer**, which opens a new story with the question already filled in, so the answer is kept forever rather than lost in a chat thread.

### Recording an elder

This is the heart of Heirloom. Many of the most valuable memories exist only in the minds of older relatives. **Record an elder** lets you sit with them, press record, and talk.

1. Choose **Record an elder**, then **New interview**. Pick who you are interviewing (add them to the tree first if needed), give the session a title, and choose the language they will speak. Hindi, Urdu and many other languages are supported.
2. Press **Start recording**. The conversation is written out on screen as they speak. Live transcription works in Chrome and Edge. In other browsers the audio is still recorded and you can type or paste the transcript afterwards.
3. A list of suggested questions sits beside the transcript. Tap one to mark where you asked it. Sit somewhere quiet, keep the phone close, follow their stories wherever they lead, and ask "who else was there?"
4. Press **Stop** when you are done. The recording and transcript are saved, and you can correct names or add anything that was missed.

**Turning the conversation into family history.** If the AI helper is on, press the extraction button and Heirloom reads the transcript in the background (this usually takes a minute or two; the page shows a note while it works and updates itself when done) and suggests: people who were mentioned (matched to those already in the tree where possible), places, dated events for the timeline, a draft story, and follow-up questions for next time. Nothing is saved until you tick the items you want and press **Add selected items to the archive**. New people suggested this way still go through the review queue like any other addition.

If the AI helper is off, you can still save the transcript and write it up as a story by hand.

### Life events

Births, marriages and deaths are recorded on profiles. Everything else in a life goes under **Life events**: a move to another city, schooling, a job, military service, a religious ceremony, an award, an illness, a celebration. Each event has a date (a year is enough), a place, and the people involved. Moves can record both where from and where to, which is what draws the migration map. Events make the timeline and each person's life timeline come alive.

## History: timeline, places and the family book

Once people, dates and memories are in, Heirloom puts them together into views of the family's past that nobody had to draw.

### Timeline

**Timeline** in the menu lays out the whole family's story decade by decade: births, marriages, deaths, moves, stories and life events, all in order. Narrow it with the **From** and **To** years, or filter to one kind of moment such as marriages only. Each person also has their own **Life timeline** on their profile.

### Places and migration

**Places & migration** shows every place the family has recorded, on a map, with a breakdown by country. Moves recorded as "Migration / move" events are drawn as journeys from one place to the next, so you can see how the family travelled from a village to a city, or from one country to another, over the generations.

A place appears on the map once it has coordinates. If a place is missing from the map, open it on this page and add its latitude and longitude. A tip: right-click a spot in any online map to copy those numbers.

### The family book

**Family book** turns the archive into a printable book. It has a chapter for each generation, a life story and photos for each person, the stories, a pedigree chart and the family timeline. Open it and use your browser's **Print** or **Save as PDF** to make a copy for a wedding, a reunion, or a grandparent who prefers paper.

### Sources & evidence

**Sources & evidence** is the family's register of what it knows and how it knows it: every fact that has been recorded with a source, plus a list of facts that still have no source. Sources can be a document, a family recollection, a recorded interview, an official record, a book, a website or a gravestone. This is the page family historians will love, and everyone else can safely ignore.

## Privacy, safety and owning your data

Heirloom's promise is simple: the archive is private, the family owns it, and you can take all of it with you at any time.

### Who can see the archive

- Unless an admin has opened the archive to visitors, only people who have been invited and have joined the family archive can see anything in it. There are no search-engine listings and no sharing with strangers.
- Photos, documents and recordings are only shown to signed-in members of that family (or to everyone, while the archive is open to visitors). Interview recordings are never shown to visitors.
- Nothing is sold, and there is no advertising.

### Extra protection for living relatives

Two settings help families that want to be careful about the living:

- **Hide living relatives from viewers and visitors.** In **Settings**, an admin can tick this box. People with the viewer role, and visitors without an account, then only see the profiles of relatives who have passed away. Contributors and above still see everyone.
- **Administrators only** on a profile. When editing a person, an admin can set *Who can see this profile* to administrators only. Use this for a sensitive branch of the family or for someone who has asked not to be visible.

### Every change is recorded

Admins can open **Audit history** to see every change ever made to the archive: who made it, when, and exactly what changed, field by field. If something looks wrong, this is where to find out what happened. The home page shows the most recent activity to every member (visitors without an account do not see it).

### Taking your data out

From **Import & export** an admin can download:

- **The complete family archive** as a single ZIP file. Press **Prepare complete archive**; packing everything takes a moment to a few minutes depending on how many photos there are, and a **Download** button appears on the same page when it is ready (the file is kept for one day or until you download it). It contains the tree in GEDCOM format, a full copy of all the data, every story as a plain text document, every original photo and file, and a README explaining what is inside. Keep a copy somewhere safe once or twice a year.
- **The family tree as a GEDCOM file.** GEDCOM is the standard format that Ancestry, MyHeritage, FamilySearch, Gramps and almost every other genealogy program can open. Second marriages, adoptions and step relationships are preserved.
- **The family book**, by opening it and saving it as a PDF.
- **A tree chart as an image**, from the Ancestors view on any profile.

### Bringing a tree in

If you already built a tree elsewhere, export it from that program as a GEDCOM file and use **Import a GEDCOM file** on the same page. Everyone in the file is added as a new person, so it is best to import into a fresh archive, or to check for duplicates afterwards.

### Family settings

Admins and the owner can change the family's name and description, choose the main language used for names and stories, and switch on the "hide living relatives" option, all under **Settings** in the menu.

### Deleting a tree

Only the owner can delete a family archive, from the bottom of **Settings** under **Delete this tree**. It removes everything at once: every person, relationship, story, event, photo, document, member and the audit history. Nothing can be brought back afterwards, so download the complete archive first if you want to keep a copy. To guard against slips, you must type the family's name before the button works. Other members simply lose access; their own accounts are untouched.

## Questions people ask

**I added a person but cannot see them in the tree.** You probably have the contributor role, so your addition is waiting in the review queue. Ask an editor or admin in the family to approve it. You will have seen a note saying "Your addition will be reviewed" when you saved.

**I only know the year someone was born. Is that a problem?** No. Type just the year, such as 1942, or a year and month, such as 1942-03. If even that is uncertain, choose *About*, *Before* or *After* next to the date.

**Two relatives disagree about a date or a name. Which one do I enter?** Both. Record each as a fact with its source under **Facts & sources** on the profile. An editor chooses which one the profile shows, and the other stays on record. Nobody has to lose the argument.

**Grandfather married twice. How do I show that?** Add each spouse as a partner from his profile, with its own type, status and dates. Then add children under the right partnership. The profile and tree will group the children under each marriage automatically.

**How do I add an adopted child or a step-parent?** When adding a parent or child, choose the relationship type: adoptive, step, foster or guardian. The chart and the "How are we related?" tool take it into account.

**We do not know one of the parents.** Add the child under the known parent and leave *Other parent* as "Unknown / not recorded". The child appears under "Children (other parent not recorded)" and you can fill in the other parent later.

**My family uses names in Hindi and Urdu as well as English.** Open the profile and use **Names in other languages** to add each version. Search finds people by any of their names, in any script. Stories can be written in any language too.

**The live transcription did not work when I recorded.** Live transcription needs Chrome or Edge. Other browsers still record the audio, and you can type or paste the transcript afterwards. The AI extraction then works the same way.

**The AI buttons say they are switched off.** The family archive's administrator has not enabled the AI helper. Everything else in Heirloom works without it; you simply write stories and tag photos yourself.

**Does the AI recognise faces in photos?** No, and it never will. It can describe a scene and guess the era, but naming the people in a photo is always done by a family member.

**Can people outside the family see our photos?** No. Only signed-in members of your family archive can open anything. A photo's web address does not work for anyone else.

**I forgot my password.** Password reset by email or text is not available yet. Ask the archive's owner or an admin for help; in the meantime, try signing in with your mobile number instead of your email, or the other way round, in case you simply mistyped.

**I did not get an invitation email.** Heirloom does not send emails yet. The person who invited you has a link to copy and send to you by WhatsApp, text or email themselves. Ask them for it.

**I belong to two families, mine and my spouse's.** Each can have its own archive. Click the family name at the top of the menu to switch, or choose **New family archive** to start another one. Your role can be different in each.

**Will the app work when I have no signal?** Pages you opened recently may still show, and you will see a short offline page otherwise. Uploading and saving need a connection.

**I imported a GEDCOM file and now some people appear twice.** Imported people are always added as new records. Check the People list for duplicates and remove the extra copies, or import into a brand-new archive next time.

**Is Heirloom free?** Yes. Creating an account and an archive costs nothing, no card is asked for, and every feature is available.
